# Secret keys and `django.utils.crypto`

Django's signing rests on one secret, `SECRET_KEY`, from which each use is given a key of its own, derived with a **salt**, a string that names the use. The secrets a site has replaced, listed in `SECRET_KEY_FALLBACKS`, still check what they signed but sign nothing. `salted_hmac` derives the key and hashes the value with it; its module, `django.utils.crypto`, also holds the comparison that checks a signature and the random strings Django's secrets are drawn from.

Django hands out values that come back to it: a signed cookie, a session kept in a cookie, the link in a password-reset email. To know such a value for its own when it returns, it attaches an HMAC, a hash of the value that only a holder of the key can compute. The salt keeps the uses apart: without it, a value the site signed for one use, passed to code that checks for another, would verify there ([Signing: `Signer`, `TimestampSigner`, `dumps` and `loads`](signing.md)). Unlike the key, the salt need not be secret (`docs/topics/signing.txt`).

## Who uses the key

`SECRET_KEY` defaults to an empty string, and reading an empty one raises `ImproperlyConfigured`, so a project without a key fails at the first thing that needs it ([The settings object](../startup/settings.md)) (`django/conf/__init__.py:LazySettings.__getattr__`). What needs it is one tree. Django's code reads the key to sign with in `Signer.__init__`, in `get_cookie_signer`, in `PasswordResetTokenGenerator.secret`, and in `salted_hmac` when it is given no other secret. Every user that signs reaches it through one of them, with a salt of its own, and every branch ends in `salted_hmac` (`django/core/signing.py:Signer.__init__`, `django/core/signing.py:get_cookie_signer`, `django/contrib/auth/tokens.py:PasswordResetTokenGenerator._get_secret`).

A `Signer` signs with its key, `SECRET_KEY` unless it is given one, and checks with that key and its fallback keys, `SECRET_KEY_FALLBACKS` unless it is given others; it reaches `salted_hmac` through `base64_hmac`, with its salt, the dotted path of its class unless it is given one, followed by `"signer"`. `dumps` and `loads` work through a `TimestampSigner`, under the salt `"django.core.signing"` unless they are given another; each may be given a key in place of `SECRET_KEY`, and `loads` old keys in place of `SECRET_KEY_FALLBACKS` ([Signing: `Signer`, `TimestampSigner`, `dumps` and `loads`](signing.md)) (`django/core/signing.py:Signer.signature`, `django/core/signing.py:dumps`). The sessions sign with `dumps`, but for the cache backend's: `SessionBase.encode` writes the stored data of the database, file and cached-database backends under the salt `"django.contrib.sessions.SessionStore"`, and the signed-cookie backend, which keeps the whole session in the cookie, signs it under `"django.contrib.sessions.backends.signed_cookies"` ([The session store: `SessionBase`](../auth/sessions.md), [The session backends](../auth/session-backends.md)) (`django/contrib/sessions/backends/base.py:SessionBase.encode`, `django/contrib/sessions/backends/signed_cookies.py:SessionStore._get_session_key`).

Cookies have a signer of their own, which `get_cookie_signer` makes of the class `SIGNING_BACKEND` names, with `b"django.http.cookies"` put before `SECRET_KEY` and before each old key. A signed cookie's salt is `"django.http.cookies.v2:"`, the length of the caller's salt, `":"`, that salt and the cookie's name ([Cookies](../http/cookies.md#signed-cookies)); the messages' `CookieStorage` signs its cookie under `"django.contrib.messages"` ([Message storage: `CookieStorage`, `SessionStorage` and `FallbackStorage`](../auth/message-storage.md)) (`django/core/signing.py:_cookie_signer_salt`, `django/contrib/messages/storage/cookie.py:CookieStorage`).

Two users call `salted_hmac` themselves. `AbstractBaseUser.get_session_auth_hash` makes the hash of the user's stored password that a session keeps, under `SECRET_KEY` with the salt `"django.contrib.auth.models.AbstractBaseUser.get_session_auth_hash"`, and `AbstractBaseUser.get_session_auth_fallback_hash` makes it again under each of `SECRET_KEY_FALLBACKS` ([The user of a request: `login`, `logout` and `AuthenticationMiddleware`](../auth/login.md#the-session-hash)). `PasswordResetTokenGenerator` makes a reset token under the salt `"django.contrib.auth.tokens.PasswordResetTokenGenerator"`, with its own `PasswordResetTokenGenerator.secret` and `PasswordResetTokenGenerator.secret_fallbacks`, which are `SECRET_KEY` and `SECRET_KEY_FALLBACKS` unless they are set ([Password reset: `PasswordResetTokenGenerator` and the reset views](../auth/password-reset.md#the-token-passwordresettokengenerator)) (`django/contrib/auth/base_user.py:AbstractBaseUser._get_session_auth_hash`, `django/contrib/auth/tokens.py:PasswordResetTokenGenerator`).

Follow a user from its leaf up to the key, and look for the old keys beside the key, which are tried only when a value is checked.

```text figure=the-keys
SECRET_KEY                                       SECRET_KEY_FALLBACKS
the key: signs, and checks first                 the old keys: check only, each in turn after the key

salted_hmac(salt, value, secret)                 the key of the HMAC: a digest of the salt and the secret

    Signer, by way of base64_hmac                salt: the signer's salt, then "signer"
        dumps and loads, a TimestampSigner       salt: "django.core.signing", unless given another
            a session's stored data              salt: "django.contrib.sessions.SessionStore"
            the signed-cookie sessions           salt: "django.contrib.sessions.backends.signed_cookies"
        get_cookie_signer                        key: b"django.http.cookies", then the key
            signed cookies                       salt: "django.http.cookies.v2:", the salt's length, ":",
                                                       the salt and the cookie's name
            the messages' CookieStorage          salt: "django.contrib.messages"
    AbstractBaseUser.get_session_auth_hash       salt: "django.contrib.auth.models.AbstractBaseUser.get_session_auth_hash"
    PasswordResetTokenGenerator                  salt: "django.contrib.auth.tokens.PasswordResetTokenGenerator"
```

*The keys Django derives from `SECRET_KEY`, as they stand by default. Each line hangs from what it calls, and the key reaches `salted_hmac` as its secret; on the right is what each line adds, a salt or, for the cookie signer, a prefix to the key, and a user's own salt takes the place of the default above it. The old keys stand beside the key, and are tried only when a value is checked.*

## Changing the key: `SECRET_KEY_FALLBACKS`

For every user, as for a `Signer` ([Signing: `Signer`, `TimestampSigner`, `dumps` and `loads`](signing.md)), the key signs and the old keys only check. `PasswordResetTokenGenerator.check_token` makes the token again under its secret and then under each of its fallbacks, and `get_user` makes the user's session hash again the same way, to compare with the one the session holds (`django/contrib/auth/tokens.py:PasswordResetTokenGenerator.check_token`, `django/contrib/auth/__init__.py:get_user`). So a value made under an old key stays good while that key is a fallback, and whatever is signed meanwhile is signed with the new one. A session whose hash was made under a fallback is kept, but `get_user` gives it a new session key, the session's id, and writes in its hash under the new `SECRET_KEY` ([The user of a request: `login`, `logout` and `AuthenticationMiddleware`](../auth/login.md#reading-the-user-back-djangocontribauthget_user)). A key is changed in two steps, then: the new key into `SECRET_KEY` and the old one to the front of `SECRET_KEY_FALLBACKS`; and later the old one out of the list, which ends the sessions, reset tokens and other values it made (`docs/ref/settings.txt`).

> **History.** Before 4.1 Django had no list of old keys. 4.1 added `SECRET_KEY_FALLBACKS`, a list of old keys for rotating the key (`docs/releases/4.1.txt`); in 4.1 a rotation under the fallbacks still invalidated the sessions, a bug fixed in 4.1.8 (`docs/releases/4.1.8.txt`).

Take the old key out of both settings, and what it made is refused, each user in its own way. `loads` raises `BadSignature`, and so does `HttpRequest.get_signed_cookie` unless it is given a default, which it then returns ([Cookies](../http/cookies.md#signed-cookies)). `CookieStorage` reads its cookie as holding no message ([Message storage: `CookieStorage`, `SessionStorage` and `FallbackStorage`](../auth/message-storage.md#a-cookie-the-client-changed)). A session's stored data is read as an empty session, with a warning to `"django.security.SuspiciousSession"` ([The session store: `SessionBase`](../auth/sessions.md#the-stored-form-encode-and-decode)), and a reset link no longer checks ([Password reset: `PasswordResetTokenGenerator` and the reset views](../auth/password-reset.md#the-token-passwordresettokengenerator)) (`django/http/request.py:HttpRequest.get_signed_cookie`, `django/contrib/messages/storage/cookie.py:CookieStorage._decode`, `django/contrib/sessions/backends/base.py:SessionBase.decode`).

A user who was logged in is logged out. Under the database backend the session's data reads as empty, so `get_user` finds no user's id in it and leaves the session under the session key it had. Under the cache backend the data is not signed and still holds the id, but the hash beside it, made under the old key, no longer matches the user's, and `get_user` flushes the session, deleting it and dropping its session key (`django/contrib/auth/__init__.py:get_user`, `django/contrib/sessions/backends/cache.py:SessionStore.load`).

Two things do not depend on the key. A password's hash is made without it, with a salt the hasher draws for it, and a change of key leaves passwords as they were (`django/contrib/auth/hashers.py:BasePasswordHasher.salt`, `docs/ref/settings.txt`). The PBKDF2 hashers, the default among them, make the hash with `pbkdf2`, which is `hashlib.pbkdf2_hmac` with SHA-256 unless another digest is given ([Password hashing: `make_password` and the hashers](../auth/hashers.md)) (`django/utils/crypto.py:pbkdf2`). The CSRF secret is a random string, made and compared without the key; in its cookie it outlasts a change of key, in the session as long as the session's data does ([The CSRF secret and its token](csrf-token.md)).

## `salted_hmac`

`salted_hmac` takes a salt, a value and a secret, which is `settings.SECRET_KEY` unless one is given. It hashes the salt followed by the secret, and uses that digest as the key of an HMAC of the value, made with the same hash; it returns Python's `hmac.HMAC`, from which the caller takes the digest in bytes or in hex. Salt, secret and value may each be a string or bytes: `force_bytes` makes them bytes (`django/utils/crypto.py:salted_hmac`). `salted_hmac` itself knows nothing of the old keys: a caller that checks a value passes each old key in turn as the secret.

The algorithm, a keyword argument, names a constructor of `hashlib`, which `salted_hmac` looks up by that name; a name `hashlib` lacks, such as `"sha257"`, raises `InvalidAlgorithm`, a `ValueError`. Given no algorithm, `salted_hmac` uses SHA-1, and warns.

> **Deprecated.** Since 6.1 a call that names no algorithm warns, and in Django 2028 the default becomes `"sha256"` (`docs/releases/6.1.txt`). Django 2028 is the release that was to be 7.0, named by its year under DEP 20, and the warning was renamed with it: RemovedInDjango70Warning in 6.1, `RemovedInDjango2028Warning` at this commit ([What still warns: the deprecations and the releases that end them](../changes/deprecations.md)) (`docs/releases/6.2.txt`, `deps:accepted/0020-annual-release-cycle.rst`). It skips Django's own files, so it is reported at the innermost line outside Django that led to the call (`django/utils/crypto.py:salted_hmac`). Django's own callers name an algorithm: `Signer` and `PasswordResetTokenGenerator` default to `"sha256"`, and the session hash passes it. `base64_hmac` has the same deprecation and warns under its own name; it passes `"sha1"` on to `salted_hmac`, so a call of it warns once (`django/core/signing.py:base64_hmac`).

## `constant_time_compare`

`constant_time_compare` makes both values bytes and returns what `secrets.compare_digest` says of them, a comparison that does not stop at the first difference: one that did would tell an attacker, by the time it took, how much of a guess was right (`django/utils/crypto.py:constant_time_compare`). It is what `Signer.unsign`, `PasswordResetTokenGenerator.check_token`, the logging in and the loading of a request's user, the CSRF check ([The CSRF check: `CsrfViewMiddleware.process_view`](csrf-check.md)) and every password hasher but one compare with; `Argon2PasswordHasher` leaves the comparison to the argon2 library (`django/contrib/auth/hashers.py:Argon2PasswordHasher.verify`).

## `get_random_string`

`get_random_string` makes a string of the length asked for, each character chosen by `secrets.choice` from the characters it is given: `RANDOM_STRING_CHARS`, the letters of both cases and the digits, unless it is given others (`django/utils/crypto.py:get_random_string`). A string's strength in bits is the base-2 logarithm of the number of strings that could have been drawn: the CSRF secret, 32 of the 62 letters and digits, is about 190 bits (`django/middleware/csrf.py:_get_new_csrf_string`). Among the other users are a session key, 32 of `VALID_KEY_CHARS`, lower-case letters and digits (`django/contrib/sessions/backends/base.py:SessionBase._get_new_session_key`), and the salts of the password hashers but the bcrypt ones (`django/contrib/auth/hashers.py:BasePasswordHasher.salt`). A new project's key is 50 characters that `get_random_secret_key` draws from lower-case letters, digits and punctuation, and the startproject command writes them into the new settings after a prefix that the deployment check of the key warns of ([The security checks](checks.md)) (`django/core/management/utils.py:get_random_secret_key`, `django/core/management/commands/startproject.py:Command.handle`).
